## The city

Saint Merdain is an original coastal metropolis rendered as an interactive city atlas. It is not a game, not a real place, and not trying to be one. It is a place for looking closer: freight yards under sodium light, hills above the marine layer, old neighborhoods that refuse to disappear, and a downtown skyline that keeps its own hours.

The project treats the map as the main character. Districts have different weather, density, architecture, and quiet little contradictions. Locations are fictional. Streets are fictional. The feeling that you have somehow been here before is intentional.

## What is inside

- A cinematic Three.js city with ocean, terrain, roads, bridges, hills, skyline, landmarks, and district overlays.
- Searchable locations with category and district filters.
- Clickable places with ratings, hours, descriptions, addresses, and nearby locations.
- Camera orbit, tilt, zoom, smooth focus movement, day/night lighting, and map layers.
- Route-planning foundations with route history and destination selection.
- Density analytics, marker clustering, spatial lookup, catalog validation, and cached geometry utilities.
- Hundreds of generated fictional places ready to grow into a larger city archive.
- A standalone browser edition in [`standalone.html`](./standalone.html) for the impatient.

## Districts

The current atlas includes industrial waterfronts, freight yards, downtown towers, old brick neighborhoods, nightlife corridors, wealthy hills, suburban edges, airport territory, and open coast. Each district is designed to feel like it has a history before the camera arrives.

## The tone

Dark, coastal, restrained. Sodium lamps. Wet asphalt. Quiet money. Late trains. A little neon where it earns its place.

Saint Merdain takes atmosphere seriously without borrowing another world’s identity. The city, its locations, and its visual language are original to this project.

## Built with

`React` · `TypeScript` · `Vite` · `Three.js` · `HTML` · `CSS`

Static by design. No backend, database, API key, or server runtime is required for the map experience.
